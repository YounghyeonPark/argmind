ADVERSARIAL CHECK 1 — which benchmark actually JUSTIFIES the bandwidth?
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Deadline margin (deadline/latency; <1.0 = miss) at 256 MACs, 16 lanes:
bench             bw32      bw64     bw128     bw256
QUAD-MPC         6.30x    11.95x    21.66x    36.46x
ARM-MPC          0.54x     0.96x     1.60x     2.38x
PWR-MPC          3.51x     5.81x     8.64x    11.41x
EKF-24         543.43x   557.05x   564.13x   567.73x
EKF-96         154.91x   156.65x   157.54x   157.99x

  ONE benchmark drives the whole bandwidth-first decision on LATENCY:
  ARM-MPC. Every other solver row has >8x deadline margin at 64 B/cycle.
  If ARM-MPC left the frozen set, 64 B/cycle would meet every solver
  deadline and the latency argument for a wide scratchpad would vanish.
  The remaining argument for width is ENERGY (shorter active window,
  cheaper per-byte access from wider bank words) -- consistent with the
  settled finding that the advantage is energy, not speed.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADVERSARIAL CHECK 2 — perf/W depends on POWER GATING between calls
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The model charges static power over the ACTIVE window only. If the CM
cannot be power-gated between calls, leakage is charged over the whole
call period and low-duty-cycle workloads collapse:
bench           duty   gated uJ  ungated uJ  perf/W gated  perf/W ungated
QUAD-MPC        4.6%       9.48       23.78         11.9x            4.8x
ARM-MPC        62.6%     287.35      298.55         15.5x           14.9x
PWR-MPC        11.6%       0.75        1.41         13.8x            7.3x
EKF-24          0.2%       0.15       15.13         21.4x            0.2x
EKF-96          0.6%       6.10       80.62         22.9x            1.7x

  Ungated, QUAD-MPC and PWR-MPC fall to single digits and EKF-24 goes
  BELOW the 5x kill criterion. Coarse-grain power gating (or at minimum
  full clock gating plus a retention-mode scratchpad) is therefore a
  REQUIREMENT of the architecture, not an optimisation. The 15 mW static
  figure is itself an [EST] and is now load-bearing: measure it in Phase 1.

ADVERSARIAL CHECK 3 — why does pJ/byte FALL as bandwidth rises?
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    64 B/cyc   8 banks x   8 B: macro 0.873 + wire 0.210 + mux 0.060 = 1.143 pJ/B
   128 B/cyc   8 banks x  16 B: macro 0.720 + wire 0.212 + mux 0.060 = 0.992 pJ/B
   256 B/cyc   8 banks x  32 B: macro 0.528 + wire 0.216 + mux 0.060 = 0.804 pJ/B
   512 B/cyc   8 banks x  64 B: macro 0.396 + wire 0.224 + mux 0.060 = 0.680 pJ/B
   128 B/cyc  16 banks x   8 B: macro 0.654 + wire 0.216 + mux 0.060 = 0.930 pJ/B
   256 B/cyc  32 banks x   8 B: macro 0.478 + wire 0.229 + mux 0.060 = 0.767 pJ/B

  Mechanism: a read activates a whole wordline, so the bitline energy is
  already spent whether you sense 64 bits or 256 of that row. Published
  45nm data [A2] confirms it -- 64b -> 512b access on the same macro is
  1.2-2x CHEAPER PER BYTE. So the prompt's hypothesis (per-byte energy
  rises with bandwidth) is WRONG for wider bank words.
  It is right about bank COUNT, but the effect is small and shows up in
  AREA (+46% scratchpad for 32 banks vs 8) far more than in energy.
  RESIDUAL RISK: if the foundry macro implements aggressive bitline
  gating (only the selected column group discharges), wide reads stop
  being cheap and pJ/byte goes FLAT with width -- our 128->256 B/cycle
  energy improvement would then be ~0 instead of -20%. This must be
  checked against the real compiler datasheet in Phase 1.

SCRATCHPAD CAPACITY — bigger is WORSE for energy (128 B/cyc, 16 banks)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    KB  spad mm2   pJ/B   QUAD-MPC    ARM-MPC    PWR-MPC     EKF-24     EKF-96   (uJ/call)
    64     0.146   0.53        7.0      197.7        0.5        0.1        5.6
   128     0.203   0.72        7.6      234.0        0.6        0.1        5.4
   256     0.319   0.93        9.0      275.0        0.7        0.2        5.9
   512     0.555   1.21       11.1      326.9        0.9        0.2        6.7
  1024     1.091   1.64       14.4      409.4        1.1        0.2        7.8

  ARM-MPC's working set is 861 KB and does NOT fit in 256 KB. Growing the
  scratchpad to hold it costs energy rather than saving it: the 622 KB
  that must stream per call needs only 0.3 GB/s (fully overlapped by
  compute, zero latency cost, ~7 uJ of DRAM energy), while 64 KB banks
  instead of 16 KB banks make EVERY scratchpad read ~1.8x more expensive
  and ARM-MPC issues ~85 MB of scratchpad reads per call.
  -> Residency of the HOT inner-loop set is what matters. Residency of the
     whole problem is not a goal, and buying it is a net energy loss.
     If a future SKU needs more capacity, it should be a SECOND TIER, not
     a wider first tier. MODEL LIMITATION: one pJ/byte is applied to all
     reads, so this model cannot yet evaluate a tiered scratchpad.
