2. WHAT THE SAME AREA BUYS IN MACs  (the decision this all turns on)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    256 MACs (bf16 mult / fp32 accum) : 0.240 mm2   |   int8-only: 0.056 mm2
    512 MACs (bf16 mult / fp32 accum) : 0.479 mm2   |   int8-only: 0.112 mm2
   1024 MACs (bf16 mult / fp32 accum) : 0.958 mm2   |   int8-only: 0.224 mm2
   4096 MACs (bf16 mult / fp32 accum) : 3.834 mm2   |   int8-only: 0.895 mm2

  Going 64 -> 128 B/cycle costs +0.042 mm2 (+15%)  = 45 bf16 MACs
  Going 64 -> 256 B/cycle costs +0.128 mm2 (+46%)  = 137 bf16 MACs
  For reference 256 -> 1024 MACs costs +0.719 mm2 and buys ZERO on
  batch-1 GEMV (settled finding, PHASE0_FINDINGS.md).

  Multi-porting instead of banking: 8T dual-port cell is ~2.1x the 6T
  single-port area [A4 cited]. Two read ports on 256 KB via a DP cell:
    ~0.582 mm2 for 2x8B/bank = 128 B/cyc, vs 0.319 mm2 for 16 single-port banks.
    -> BANKING WINS ON AREA BY ~2x. Multi-porting is also worse on
       energy (longer bitlines, more per-access periphery) and on
       timing. Decision: bank, never multi-port. Confidence: high.

  VALIDITY LIMIT: rows marked EXTRAPOLATED have a bank word narrower than
  64 bits, below which no published energy data exists. The model clamps
  and therefore keeps predicting ever-cheaper bytes; physically pJ/byte
  must turn back UP as per-access fixed costs stop amortising. DESIGN RULE:
  bank word >= 64 bits, i.e. bank count <= (bytes/cycle)/8.

  READ THIS CAREFULLY — the naive intuition in the task prompt is WRONG:
  macro-internal energy per byte FALLS with more banks (shorter bitlines,
  fewer rows precharged) and FALLS with wider per-bank words (fixed decode
  amortised over more bits). [A2] is unambiguous on both.
  What RISES with bandwidth is the DISTRIBUTION network: a physically
  larger scratchpad region and a wider bus mean more wire energy per byte.
  That term is absent from the current model, and at 16nm it is comparable
  to the macro read itself.

4. DELIVERED vs PEAK BANDWIDTH — short GEMV rows (fp32, 4 B/elem)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
workload                           bw64            bw128            bw256
                            naive   seg      naive   seg      naive   seg
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
PWR-MPC   n_var=30          60       64     120      128     120      256
QUAD-MPC  n_var=80          64       64     107      128     160      256
ARM-MPC   n_var=210         60       64     120      128     210      256
EKF-24    n=24              48       64      96      128      96      256
EKF-96    n=96              64       64     128      128     192      256
SLM row   n=2048            64       64     128      128     256      256

  'naive' = one dot product per fetch beat; the tail beat is wasted.
  'seg'   = segmented reduction: one beat may straddle two output rows.
  Without segmented reduction, 256 B/cycle delivers as little as 47% of
  peak on PWR-MPC and 62% on QUAD-MPC. The model's bandwidth sweep
  assumes 100%. SEGMENTED REDUCTION IS A REQUIRED FEATURE, not an
  optimisation — without it, roughly a third of the bandwidth you paid
  area and power for is unreachable on the beachhead workload.

5. BANK CONFLICTS — naive A.T@y column walk (fraction of peak achieved)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
workload                    8bx8B     16bx8B     32bx8B    16bx16B    32bx16B
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
PWR-MPC   n_var=30           100%       100%       100%       100%       100%
QUAD-MPC  n_var=80            12%        12%        12%        25%        25%
ARM-MPC   n_var=210          100%       100%       100%       100%       100%
EKF-24    n=24                25%        25%        25%        50%        50%
EKF-96    n=96                12%         6%         6%        12%        12%
SLM row   n=2048              12%         6%         3%         6%         3%

  Sequential row reads (P@x, A@x, cached triangular solve) are
  CONFLICT-FREE BY CONSTRUCTION with address-interleaved banks — the
  dominant solver pattern is fine, which is the good news.
  A.T@y walked as columns is NOT: on QUAD-MPC (n_var=80, 320 B stride)
  a 32-bank x 8B organisation lands on 4 of 32 banks -> 12.5% of peak.
  FIX IS FREE AND IN SOFTWARE: A.T@y = sum_i A[i,:].T * y_i is
  row-sequential. Cost is one n_var-length fp32 accumulator (320 B for
  QUAD-MPC, 840 B for ARM-MPC) held in the vector RF, not a transposed
  copy of A. This is a CODEGEN OBLIGATION to write down in the MACIF /
  codegen contract, and a reason the number of banks should not be a
  power of two that divides common matrix strides. Prefer XOR-swizzled
  bank indexing as belt-and-braces (deterministic, statically known).

6. RECOMMENDED CM-100 BASELINE
======================================================================================================
  Scratchpad : 256 KB, 16 single-port banks x 8 B/cycle = 128 B/cycle
               256 rows x 512 cols, 8:1 col mux, 16 KB/bank
  MAC array  : 256 MACs (16x16), bf16/fp16 mult + fp32 accumulate,
               int8 double-pumped, fp32 by 2-pass decomposition
  Vector unit: 16 fp32 lanes (up from 8: past ~128 B/cycle the ADMM
               projection/transcendental work becomes the co-bottleneck)
  Frequency  : 800 MHz

  Scratchpad area      0.319 mm2  (6.6 Mb/mm2, 46% array efficiency)
  MAC array area       0.240 mm2
  Vector unit          0.009 mm2  [EST 2.2k gates/lane]
  Sequencer + descr.   0.045 mm2  [EST]
  DMA + AXI/ACE-Lite   0.070 mm2  [EST]
  misc (CSR, MBIST..)  0.030 mm2  [EST]

  Subtotal             0.713 mm2
  +15% top-level route/CTS/margin      -> 0.820 mm2 TOTAL [EST]
