============================================================================================
DESCRIPTOR EMIT COST — closing the seq_setup_cycles [EST] by counting RV32
============================================================================================
Descriptor format v0: 8 words / 32 bytes.
RV32IM at 1 IPC, write-buffered descriptor queue. Optimistic by design.

                                          case  desc/tmpl    naive    delta    hwgen
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
        HPIPM stage sweep, 1 shape x 30 stages      540.0     23.0     10.0     0.04
ADMM iteration, QUAD-MPC (28 desc / ~6 shapes)        4.7     23.0     10.0     4.71
 ADMM iteration, PWR-MPC (16 desc / ~6 shapes)        2.7     23.0     10.0     8.25

  Instruction counts: naive 23, delta 10, generator arming 22 (once per template).

  BREAK-EVEN: a template must cover >= 5.5 descriptors — call it 6 — for
  the generator to beat the assumed 4 cycles/descriptor.

============================================================================================
VERDICT on seq_setup_cycles = 4
============================================================================================
  Assumed: 4.0 cycles/descriptor, unconditionally.
  The count says it is achievable, but ONLY under two conditions, and the
  second one is new:

  1. A hardware descriptor generator is required. Software emit cannot get
     near 4 at any encoding: naive is 23 cycles/descriptor and even a
     delta-encoded queue is 10. So the assumption silently presumed the
     generator — the same conclusion the sequencer analysis reached from the
     other direction (run-ahead is mandatory). Two independent routes to it.

  2. Templates must cover >= 6 descriptors. This is the part the flat
     assumption hid. Arming costs ~22 instructions ONCE PER TEMPLATE, so the
     benefit depends on descriptors-per-template, not on total descriptors:
       HPIPM stage sweep    0.04 cycles/desc — 130x inside the bar
       ADMM iteration       8.25 cycles/desc — ABOVE 4, i.e. the
                                 assumption FAILS for ADMM as decomposed

  The ADMM result is the finding. ADMM issues ~6 distinct kernel shapes per
  iteration and only 16-28 descriptors, so each template covers 3-5 of them
  and arming is not amortised. Two ways out, both codegen-side:
    * Hoist arming out of the iteration loop. The shapes are IDENTICAL every
      iteration — only the iterate values change, not addresses or dimensions
      — so templates can be armed ONCE PER SOLVE and left resident, making
      the per-iteration cost ~0. This is available precisely because
      allocation is static, and it is the same property that makes overlap
      legal. Worth designing the generator to hold several armed templates.
    * Failing that, fuse the vector-update and projection descriptors, which
      cuts shape count rather than descriptor count.

  Independent corroboration of the model's pessimistic case: the sequencer
  analysis charged write-descriptor-and-poll at 'setup ~20' cycles and found
  it misses PWR-MPC's budget. This count gives 23 for exactly that
  design, derived from instructions rather than intuition. They agree.

  Delta encoding is NOT worth it: it needs an extra queue-protocol word and a
  shadow descriptor register file, and still lands well above the generator.
  Drop it from consideration.

  ACTION: the generator needs a multi-template capability (hold >= 6 armed
  templates), and that requirement did not exist before this count.

REMAINING [EST] IN THIS COUNT
  * 1 IPC with no I-fetch stalls and a never-full queue. A real RV32 core
    with a small I-mem will be somewhat worse on the software paths, which
    widens the gap in the generator's favour.
  * Descriptors/kernel (540 / 28 / 16) come from the sequencer analysis'
    kernel decomposition, which is itself [DER], not measured.
  * Assumes three independent base addresses suffice. A kernel needing a
    fourth (e.g. separate bias or scaling vector) would add one AGU.
