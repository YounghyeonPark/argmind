==========================================================================================
CPU BACKEND MEASUREMENT — how much SIMD headroom is recoverable in software?
==========================================================================================
MEASURED on this x86 host (i9-13900HX, AVX2+FMA, no AVX-512), single core,
fp64, OpenBLAS pinned to 1 thread. Absolute microseconds do NOT transfer to
the model's Arm baselines; achieved fraction of peak does, as headroom.

Problem: coupled QUAD-MPC, TIGHT bounds (active set present).
  n=80 decision vars, Gamma 240x80, 320 constraint rows, 22 ADMM iterations, rho=0.125 (power of two, ruling R5)

Empirical single-core fp64 peak (512^3 DGEMM, OpenBLAS): 224.9 GFLOP/s
  Used as the ceiling. Generous on purpose -- a large in-cache DGEMM is the
  best case for this machine, so every fraction below is conservative.

==========================================================================================
WHAT THIS SETTLES
==========================================================================================
  1. SOFTWARE HEADROOM IS REAL BUT BOUNDED.
     The same 22 iterations: tuned C 93.6 us vs OSQP 283.0 us = 3.02x.
     numpy/OpenBLAS takes 1201 us -- 12.8x SLOWER than the plain C loop,
     despite calling a tuned BLAS. At n=80 the per-call dispatch
     overhead of a BLAS routine exceeds the arithmetic it performs.

  2. AND NOBODY GETS NEAR PEAK, WHICH IS THE REAL RESULT.
     Best dense achieved: 21.0 GFLOP/s = 9.3% of this machine's
     own fp64 DGEMM peak (225 GFLOP/s). The model assumes 25-35%.
     So the model's CPU baseline is OPTIMISTIC by roughly 3x on this
     workload. Note the direction: correcting it makes our accelerator
     look BETTER, which is exactly the kind of correction to distrust
     until it is measured on Arm rather than inferred from x86.

  3. WHY IT CANNOT REACH PEAK -- THIS CONFIRMS THE CENTRAL FINDING.
     The limit is not the FMA units. It is operand fetch: Gamma-transpose v
     and Gamma x read every matrix element exactly once with no reuse, and
     the two triangular solves are loop-carried and cannot vectorise across
     rows. That is precisely the settled finding -- batch-1 solver work is
     operand-fetch-bound, not compute-bound -- now MEASURED on a CPU instead
     of modelled on our datapath.
     Consequence for the thesis: a GPU or NPU fails here for the SAME
     reason. On this workload SIMD is not 'half-hearted next to a GPU' --
     they are all in the same operand-fetch regime, and the CPU is the one
     that at least has the control plane for free.

LIMITS
  * n=80 with an 80x80 factor and a 240x80 Gamma: the whole working set is
    ~250 KB and fits L2. A cache-resident problem is the FRIENDLY case for
    the CPU, so this understates the accelerator's advantage rather than
    overstating it.
  * Single core. A CPU can throw more cores at throughput; it cannot throw
    them at one loop-carried solve, which is the architecture's premise.
  * fp64. The model's Arm baselines are fp32 for ADMM rows.
  * Measures TIME, not ENERGY. The perf/W claim is untouched by this: same
    silicon at the same voltage, so software cannot buy a structural energy
    win. Determinism is likewise untouched.
